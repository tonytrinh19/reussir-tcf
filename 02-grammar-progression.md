### 1. Pronominal verbs (verbes pronominaux)
**Why it matters:** Your #1 flagged systematic error. It touches nearly every past-tense sentence you write, so it multiplies across everything else in this document.
**Already know (B1):** Present-tense reflexive conjugation (*je me lève, tu te lèves*).
**Need to master:** Passé composé with *être* + agreement of the past participle with the reflexive pronoun (*elle s'est levée*) — **except** when a direct object follows, which blocks agreement (*elle s'est lavé les mains*, not *lavée*, because *les mains* is the direct object). Negation wraps both parts (*elle ne s'est pas levée*). Inherently pronominal verbs (*se rendre compte, s'apercevoir, se souvenir*) must never drop the reflexive pronoun.
**Common mistakes (yours):** Dropping "*s'*" on inherently pronominal verbs — *nous avons rendu compte* instead of *nous nous sommes rendu compte* [we realized]; auxiliary/agreement slips; reflexive pronoun dropped on *s'aider de* / *s'inquiéter*.
**Examples:** *Elle s'est levée à 7h.* [She got up at 7am.] · *Nous nous sommes rendu compte de l'erreur.* [We realized the mistake — *rendu* stays invariable; *compte* is the direct object.]
**Exercise:** Daily 10-sentence present→passé composé transformation drill, rotating through both regular reflexives and inherently pronominal verbs.
**Mastery criterion:** Zero auxiliary/agreement errors across 2 consecutive real writing submissions, plus 9/10 on the isolated drill.

### 2. Subjonctif présent
**Why it matters:** Your current stated target and a hard B2 marker — TCF graders look for it specifically after volition, necessity, emotion, doubt, and certain conjunctions.
**Already know (B1):** Indicative forms; likely passive recognition of the subjunctive.
**Need to master:** Formation (off the 3rd-person-plural indicative stem, irregular stems for *être/avoir/aller/faire/pouvoir/savoir/vouloir*); the core trigger list — *il faut que, vouloir que, avoir peur que, douter que, bien que, avant que, afin que, pour que, à moins que, sans que, jusqu'à ce que*.
**Common mistakes (yours):** Conditionnel substituted for subjonctif specifically after *afin que* / *avant que* — this is your most concrete, named gap.
**Examples:** *Il faut que tu viennes.* [You need to come.] · *Bien qu'il soit fatigué, il continue.* [Although he's tired, he keeps going.] · ✗ *afin qu'il pourrait* → ✓ *afin qu'il puisse* [so that he can].
**Exercise:** Contrastive drill — same sentence stem, alternate subjonctif-trigger vs. non-trigger versions, and say the mood choice out loud before writing it.
**Mastery criterion:** 12/15+ on a mixed subjonctif/indicatif/conditionnel drill.

### 3. Séquence des temps (imparfait / passé composé / plus-que-parfait)
**Why it matters:** Already drilled once, scoring 6/10 — specifically weak on anteriority that isn't flagged with an explicit "before."
**Already know (B1):** Basic passé composé and imparfait.
**Need to master:** Imparfait = background/ongoing/habitual; passé composé = completed punctual action; plus-que-parfait = anterior to another past action, **even when nothing in the sentence says "before."**
**Common mistakes (yours):** Passé composé used where plus-que-parfait was needed, because the anteriority was implied rather than stated.
**Examples:** *Quand je suis arrivé, elle était déjà partie.* [When I arrived, she had already left — *était partie* is anterior to *arrivé*.]
**Exercise:** Take a simple past-tense paragraph and mark which verbs are actually anterior to another past event based on the story logic, not on spotting the word "avant."
**Mastery criterion:** 8/10+ on a séquence des temps drill (up from your 6/10 baseline).

### 4. Si-clauses (hypothesis)
**Why it matters:** Core B2 argumentation structure — shows up constantly once you're comparing viewpoints or speculating (Tâche 3, Orale Tâche 3).
**Already know (B1):** Type 1 — *si* + présent + futur (real/likely condition).
**Need to master:** Type 2 — *si* + imparfait + conditionnel présent (hypothetical present); Type 3 — *si* + plus-que-parfait + conditionnel passé (counterfactual past).
**Common mistakes (yours):** Infinitive used instead of a conjugated verb after *si* — *si on vivre* instead of *si on vivait/vit*.
**Examples:** *Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais davantage.* [If I had more time, I'd travel more.] · *Si j'avais su, je ne serais pas venu.* [If I had known, I wouldn't have come.]
**Exercise:** Complete-the-sentence drill mixing all three types.
**Mastery criterion:** 9/10 on a mixed-type si-clause drill.

### 5. Relative pronouns (qui / que / où / dont / lequel / auquel)
**Why it matters:** Lets you embed clauses instead of stacking short sentences — one of the fastest ways to sound B2 rather than B1.
**Already know (B1):** Basic *qui/que*.
**Need to master:** *dont* (replaces *de* + noun), *où* (place/time), *lequel/auquel/duquel* and their forms (after prepositions, replacing clunky prepositional phrases).
**Common mistakes (yours):** None flagged yet specifically — but your writing likely under-uses these relative to your B1 habit of short, separate sentences.
**Examples:** *Le livre dont je te parle...* [The book I'm telling you about...] · *La ville où j'habite...* [The town where I live...] · *Le projet auquel je pense...* [The project I'm thinking about...]
**Exercise:** Sentence-combining drill — merge two short sentences using the correct relative pronoun.
**Mastery criterion:** Correctly combine 8/10 sentence pairs.

### 6. Object pronouns + y / en
**Why it matters:** Needed for concise, natural B2 register; avoids the repetition that reads as B1.
**Already know (B1):** Basic *le/la/les*.
**Need to master:** *lui/leur* (indirect); *y* (replaces *à* + place/thing); *en* (replaces *de* + noun/quantity); pronoun order when stacking (*me le, te la*); placement with infinitives and the imperative.
**Common mistakes (yours):** Overlaps with the imperative+pronoun issue below.
**Examples:** *J'y pense souvent.* [I think about it often.] · *Il en a trois.* [He has three of them.]
**Exercise:** Substitution drill — rewrite sentences replacing the noun phrase with the correct pronoun.
**Mastery criterion:** 9/10 on the substitution drill.

### 7. Embedded / indirect questions
**Why it matters:** A directly flagged recurring error — comes up whenever you report a question or ask something inside a larger sentence. (This is exactly the direct-vs-embedded distinction from your earlier question-formation practice this session — same underlying logic, different context.)
**Already know (B1):** Direct-question inversion.
**Need to master:** Embedded questions drop inversion entirely and go back to normal subject–verb order; a conjugated verb is required, never an infinitive.
**Common mistakes (yours):** *pourquoi je sortir* instead of *pourquoi je sors*; inversion incorrectly carried into an embedded clause — *quand serai-je* instead of *quand je serai*.
**Examples:** *Je me demande pourquoi il est parti.* [I wonder why he left.] · *Elle ne sait pas quand je serai là.* [She doesn't know when I'll be there.]
**Exercise:** Transformation drill — convert direct questions into embedded ones after *Je me demande.../Il ne sait pas...*
**Mastery criterion:** 9/10 on the transformation drill.

### 8. Imperative + object pronoun placement
**Why it matters:** Flagged as persisting since your very first session — the writing-and-correcting loop hasn't extinguished it, so it needs a different practice mode, not more exposure.
**Already know (B1):** Basic imperative forms.
**Need to master:** Affirmative imperative — pronoun goes *after* the verb with a hyphen, and *me/te* become *moi/toi* (*envoie-moi, donne-le-moi*). Negative imperative — pronoun stays *before* the verb (*ne me l'envoie pas*).
**Common mistakes (yours):** *m'envoie* instead of *envoie-moi* — unchanged since session one.
**Exercise:** Isolated flashcard-style rapid drill, deliberately kept *separate* from longer writing tasks — 5 minutes daily until it's boring.
**Mastery criterion:** 10/10 on the isolated drill, sustained across 3 separate days (one clean pass doesn't count — this one needs to actually stick).

### 9. Elision
**Why it matters:** Mechanical, but recurring — *je achèterai* / *que habiter*-type misses show up repeatedly.
**Need to master:** *je/me/te/se/le/la/ne/que/de* → *j'/m'/t'/s'/l'/n'/qu'/d'* before a vowel or mute *h*.
**Common mistakes (yours):** Same pattern flagged more than once — elision missed before vowel-initial words.
**Exercise:** A dedicated proofreading pass on every submission — read once *only* hunting for missed elisions, nothing else.
**Mastery criterion:** Zero missed elisions across 2 consecutive submissions.

### 10. Cause / consequence connectors
**Why it matters:** Needed for structured argumentation in Tâche 3 and Orale Tâche 3.
**Need to master:** *parce que/car/puisque* for cause (*car* is more written/formal; *puisque* implies an already-known reason); *donc/c'est pourquoi/par conséquent/d'où* for consequence.
**Examples:** *Puisque tu es là, autant en profiter.* [Since you're here, might as well make the most of it.] · *Il pleuvait, par conséquent nous sommes restés à la maison.* [It was raining, so we stayed home.]
**Exercise:** Rewrite a paragraph, using a different cause/consequence connector each time without changing the meaning.
**Mastery criterion:** Vary connectors correctly across a 150-word paragraph with no repeats.

### 11. Concession / opposition
**Why it matters:** The clearest B2 signal in argumentation — shows you can hold two sides of an issue instead of just asserting one. Directly needed for Tâche 3's "compare both viewpoints" requirement.
**Need to master:** *bien que/quoique* + subjonctif; *alors que/tandis que* (contrast); *malgré* + noun; *cependant/néanmoins/toutefois/en revanche* as connectors.
**Examples:** *Bien que je comprenne son point de vue, je ne suis pas d'accord.* [Although I understand his point of view, I disagree.]
**Exercise:** "Yes, but" paragraph drill — state a position, concede the opposite with *bien que/alors que*, then pivot back to your view.
**Mastery criterion:** One paragraph correctly using at least 2 different concession structures.

### 12. Conditionnel
**Why it matters:** Covers politeness register, hypothesis results (pairs with si-clauses above), and reporting unconfirmed information.
**Need to master:** Formation (infinitive + imperfect endings, same irregular stems as futur simple); the three uses — politeness (*je voudrais*), hypothesis result, and reported/unconfirmed info (*il serait déjà parti* — apparently).
**Examples:** *Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.* [I'd like a coffee, please.] · *Selon la rumeur, il serait déjà parti.* [Rumor has it he's already left.]
**Exercise:** Register-shift drill — rewrite a blunt request as a polite conditionnel one.
**Mastery criterion:** 9/10 on formation + correctly identifying which use-case applies.

### 13. Reported speech (discours rapporté)
**Why it matters:** Requested topic, and directly useful for Tâche 3's summary-of-two-documents requirement — reporting what a document said *is* reported speech.
**Need to master:** Tense backshift (présent→imparfait, passé composé→plus-que-parfait, futur→conditionnel); pronoun/possessive shifts; time-expression shifts (*aujourd'hui→ce jour-là, demain→le lendemain*).
**Examples:** Direct: *"Je viendrai demain."* → Reported: *Il a dit qu'il viendrait le lendemain.* [He said he would come the next day.]
**Exercise:** Direct→reported transformation drill, checking both the verb tense and the time expression each time.
**Mastery criterion:** 8/10 on the transformation drill.

### 14. Passive voice
**Why it matters:** Requested topic; useful for the impersonal/formal register that suits Tâche 3-style writing.
**Need to master:** *être* + past participle (+ *par/de* for the agent); recognizing when French actually prefers *on* over a true passive (much more often than English does).
**Examples:** *Le rapport a été rédigé par l'équipe.* [The report was written by the team] — vs. the more natural *On a rédigé le rapport.*
**Exercise:** Active→passive transformation, then a second pass asking "would *on* sound more natural here?"
**Mastery criterion:** Correctly transform 8/10, and correctly judge when *on* is the better idiomatic choice.

### 15. Gérondif + participles
**Why it matters:** Requested topic; adds simultaneity or manner without a second clause — compact and distinctly B2.
**Need to master:** *en* + present participle for simultaneity/manner (*en marchant*); present participle as adjective (*une décision surprenante*); past participle as adjective, with normal agreement.
**Examples:** *Il a répondu en souriant.* [He answered while smiling.] · *Une histoire fascinante.* [A fascinating story.]
**Exercise:** Combine two simultaneous actions from a short prompt into one gérondif sentence.
**Mastery criterion:** 8/10 on the combination drill.

### 16. Micro-error checklist (lexical, not grammatical systems — drill these with spaced-repetition flashcards, not dedicated exercises)

| Confusion | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *c'est* vs. *il/elle est* | *c'est* + article/noun; *il/elle est* + bare adjective/profession | *C'est un bon médecin* vs. *Il est médecin* |
| Prepositions + places | *à* + city; *en* + feminine country; *au* + masculine country; *aux* + plural country | *à Milan, en France, au Canada, aux États-Unis* |
| *seul* vs. *seulement* | *seul* = alone/adjective; *seulement* = only/adverb | *Il est seul.* vs. *J'ai seulement deux minutes.* |
| *sûr* vs. *sauf* | *sûr* = sure/certain; *sauf* = except | *Je suis sûr.* vs. *Tout le monde, sauf lui.* |
| *le tour* vs. *la tour* | *le tour* = a turn/trip; *la tour* = a tower | *le tour du monde* vs. *la tour Eiffel* |
| False cognates from English | "having a good time" ≠ *avoir un bon temps* | Use *s'amuser / passer un bon moment* instead |
| *les* vs. *des* | *les* = definite (specific/generic); *des* = indefinite plural | *J'aime les chats.* vs. *J'ai des chats.* |
| *leur* vs. *ses* | *leur* = their (multiple owners, one thing); *ses* = his/her (one owner) | *leur maison* (their house) vs. *ses enfants* (his/her kids) |

## Low priority — don't spend production time here

- **Passé simple:** Recognition only, as you've already noted. You'll meet it in literary/journalistic CE passages (learn to recognize *il fut, il eut, il vint, il fit*) — you'll essentially never need to *produce* it in a TCF task.
- **Subjonctif imparfait / plus-que-parfait:** Literary register, effectively extinct outside very formal writing. Skip.
- **Passé antérieur:** Formal literary narrative tense. Skip.
- **Style-soutenu front-inversions** beyond ordinary question formation (e.g., *"Peut-être viendra-t-il"*): fine to recognize while reading, not worth practicing to produce.
- **Rare/archaic connectors** (*nonobstant, partant, derechef*): outside TCF register entirely.
