## Part 1 — Where you actually are, and what B2 actually means

### 1.1 What separates B1 from B2 (and where you already sit)

B1 French is: simple and compound sentences, basic connectors (*et, mais, parce que*), narration in one clear timeframe at a time, and communication that works but breaks under complexity. B2 French is: reliable subordination (subjonctif, hypothèses, concession), the ability to argue a position with structure rather than just stating an opinion, register control (knowing when to be formal vs. casual), and — critically — being able to do all of that *while also* handling abstract or societal topics, not just concrete personal ones.

Your profile doesn't fit the usual B1 description. Your notes show you already *reaching* for B2 structures — attempting subjonctif, building longer argumentative paragraphs — but misfiring on the mechanics underneath them (pronominal verb agreement, elision, embedded question word order). That's actually a better position than "hasn't tried B2 structures yet." The fix isn't teaching you new grammar concepts; it's making the grammar you're already attempting *automatic* instead of effortful, so it survives contact with a 60-minute timed writing test. Do not simplify your ambition to make the errors go away — close the gap the other direction.

### 1.2 General French B2 vs. TCF Canada B2 — these are genuinely different targets

**General B2 (CEFR)** is about real-world communicative competence: can you function, argue, and understand nuance in French with no formal constraints on how you do it.

**TCF Canada B2 (NCLC 7)** is that same competence, but demonstrated inside a rigid, repeatable task format, under a strict clock, scored by rubric — and with specific compliance failures that tank your score regardless of how good your French actually is. France Éducation International states outright that a written task can be marked **"A1 non atteint"** (A1 not reached) if the word count isn't respected, the writing is illegible, or the response is off-topic — independent of grammar quality.

This is not a hypothetical for you. Your last two concrete data points are exactly this kind of compliance failure, not grammar failure:
- Tâche 3 (comparing two viewpoints + giving your opinion) has twice **skipped the required comparison of the two source documents** and gone straight to personal opinion — a structural miss, not a grammar one.
- The most recent Tâche 3 attempt landed around **220 words against a 180-word maximum** — the previous attempt undershot the 120-word minimum. You've now missed in both directions, which points to "not tracking words while writing" more than "can't estimate length."

Both of these are 100% fixable with template discipline (Section 7 of Document 4) and have nothing to do with how advanced your grammar is. Fix them early — they're cheap wins.

### 1.3 Where deliberate practice needs to go, specifically for you

1. **Listening, Reading, and Speaking start from zero structured practice.** Everything in your history so far — the subjonctif work, the séquence des temps [sequence of tenses] drill, the Tâche 1/2/3 writing — is Expression Écrite. That's a full three of four TCF skills with no dedicated practice yet. This isn't a problem *now*, three weeks in — it's normal to go deep on one skill first. It becomes a problem if the roadmap keeps deferring the other three. The phase plan below front-loads them starting Phase 1, specifically so writing doesn't quietly eat the whole study budget the way it tends to for people who like writing (or who have a working correction loop already built, which you do).
2. **A handful of errors are persisting *despite* being flagged before** — imperative + object pronoun inversion (*envoie-moi*, not *m'envoie*) is flagged as recurring "since the very first session," and elision (*j'achèterai*, not *je achèterai*) shows the same repeat pattern. Writing-and-correcting isn't extinguishing these on its own. They need a different practice mode: short, isolated, high-repetition transformation drills (see Document 2), done daily until they're boring, not folded into longer writing tasks where they can hide.
3. **Grammar-under-time-pressure is the real B2 test**, not grammar-in-isolation. You can produce subjonctif; the gap shows up specifically after triggers like *afin que* / *avant que*, where conditionnel gets substituted instead. That's a contrastive-drill problem (subjonctif vs. conditionnel side by side), not a "learn the subjunctive" problem — you already know it exists.

### 1.4 What B2 looks like on the TCF Canada scoreboard

TCF Canada reports each of the four skills on its own scale, converted by IRCC to an NCLC (Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens) level — the French-language version of CLB. **IRCC scores every skill independently and never averages them.** A 520 in listening does not offset a 440 in reading; your weakest skill is your real result for that skill. Keep this in view all the way through the roadmap — it's why Phase 5 ends by hunting your single lowest skill, not by admiring your best one.

| NCLC (= CLB) | CEFR | Listening (CO) | Reading (CE) | Writing (EE) | Speaking (EO) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10+ | C1–C2 | 549–699 | 549–699 | 16–20 | 16–20 |
| 9 | C1 | 523–548 | 524–548 | 14–15 | 14–15 |
| 8 | B2 | 503–522 | 499–523 | 12–13 | 12–13 |
| **7 (your target)** | **B2** | **458–502** | **453–498** | **10–11** | **10–11** |
| 6 | B1 | 398–457 | 406–452 | 7–8 | 7–8 |
| 5 | B1 | 369–397 | 375–405 | 6 | 6 |
| 4 | A2 | 331–368 | 342–374 | 4–5 | 4–5 |

Your existing targets (Listening 458–502, Reading 453–498, Writing/Speaking 10–11) are exactly the **NCLC 7 band** — confirmed against IRCC's current published table. NCLC 7 in all four skills is the standard minimum for the French-language-proficiency Express Entry category and its CRS bonus, so aiming for a clean 10–11/458/453 across the board (rather than overshooting into NCLC 8+ territory you don't need) is a sound, efficient target — put spare effort into your *weakest* skill instead of polishing an already-sufficient one.

One current, exam-mechanics note worth knowing going in: as of the September 1, 2026 test sessions, **re-marking requests (demandes de recorrection) for the Expression Écrite and Expression Orale sections are not currently being accepted**, for roughly a year. Treat every full mock in Phase 4–5 as if it's the one real attempt — there's less of a safety net right now than there sometimes has been.

## Part 2 — The Roadmap: B1 → NCLC 7 in ~20 weeks

Five phases, ~20–21 weeks at your stated 1.5–2 h/day, 6 days/week pace. Each phase has a **measurable exit test** — don't advance on a calendar; advance when the criteria are met. If a phase runs long, that's real information (it's telling you where the actual bottleneck is), not failure.

| Phase | Weeks | Core theme |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation Repair | 1–3 | Stop the recurring mechanical errors before building more on top of them; establish CO/CE/EO baselines |
| 2. B1+ Consolidation | 4–8 | Close remaining B1 gaps; bring Listening/Reading/Speaking from zero to functional |
| 3. B2 Development | 9–14 | Build the real B2 grammar toolkit; first full-skill attempts at B2 ceiling |
| 4. B2 + TCF Specialization | 15–18 | Convert general B2 ability into exam-format reliability under real conditions |
| 5. Mocks & Final Optimization | 19–21 | Full timed dress rehearsals; triage your single weakest skill |

### Phase 1 — Foundation Repair (Weeks 1–3)

- **Objective:** Eliminate the errors that are actively undermining more advanced structures, and get a first honest read on Listening/Reading/Speaking.
- **Grammar:** Pronominal verbs at passé composé (*être* + reflexive agreement — your #1 flagged error); négation with reflexives (*on ne s'est pas vus*); elision; imperative + object pronoun order (*envoie-moi*); embedded/indirect questions (*pourquoi je sors*, not *pourquoi je sortir*; *quand je serai*, not *quand serai-je*); subjonctif présent formation + top triggers.
- **Vocabulary:** Daily life, connectors (*mais, donc, alors, cependant, par contre*), immigration/admin basics (they recur across topics).
- **Listening:** One untimed diagnostic sample; start 10–15 min/day of slow-paced audio purely for exposure and calibration — no target score yet.
- **Reading:** One diagnostic TCF-style Compréhension écrite (CE) set to find your real comfortable level.
- **Speaking:** Record yourself cold doing Orale Tâche 1 (*se présenter* — introduce yourself) as a baseline. No pressure; just a start point.
- **Writing:** Continue Tâche 1/2; add a mandatory word-count check before every submission.
- **TCF-specific:** Learn the exact official format of all four épreuves cold, and the four "A1 non atteint" auto-fail triggers (illegible, wrong word count, off-topic, task skipped), so none of them is ever hit by accident.
- **Exit criteria:** Zero pronominal-verb auxiliary errors across 2 consecutive submissions · 9/10+ on a dedicated imperative+pronoun drill · subjonctif présent correctly formed (regular + top irregulars) in isolation · one baseline recording/CO sample/CE sample on file.

### Phase 2 — B1+ Consolidation (Weeks 4–8)

- **Objective:** Close the remaining B1 gaps; get Listening, Reading, and Speaking into a real weekly rhythm; deepen subjonctif.
- **Grammar:** Séquence des temps (imparfait vs. passé composé; plus-que-parfait for anteriority — your drilled weak spot); si-clauses type 1 & 2; relative pronouns *qui/que/où/dont*; direct/indirect object pronouns; cause/consequence connectors; the recurring micro-errors (*c'est* vs. *il/elle est*, prepositions with cities/countries, possessive agreement, article choice — see Document 2's checklist).
- **Vocabulary:** Work/employment, education, health, family/society (~15–20 new items/day with spaced repetition — system in Document 4).
- **Listening:** TCF CO-style MCQ sets, 3×/week, gradually increasing pace.
- **Reading:** TCF CE-style MCQ sets, 3×/week.
- **Speaking:** Begin Orale Tâche 1 & 2 practice (fluent self-intro; asking questions to get information in a role-play); record and self-review weekly.
- **Writing:** Continue Tâche 1/2; formally introduce Tâche 3's real structure — compare **both** source documents before giving your own opinion — as a template you follow every single time, not something you improvise.
- **TCF-specific:** Track every writing submission against the real caps (Tâche 1: 60–120 words · Tâche 2: 120–150 · Tâche 3: 120–180).
- **Exit criteria:** Séquence des temps drill at 8/10+ (up from your 6/10 baseline) · a Tâche 3 draft that explicitly references both documents before the opinion section, within word limits, in one sitting · CO/CE diagnostics at 70%+ on B1-level material · a fluent, hesitation-light Orale Tâche 1 recording (~1 min).

### Phase 3 — B2 Development (Weeks 9–14)

- **Objective:** Build the actual B2 grammar toolkit and take a first genuine run at B2 ceiling across all four skills.
- **Grammar:** Subjonctif expansion (doubt/emotion/opinion triggers; contrastive drills specifically pitting subjonctif against conditionnel after *afin que/avant que* — your known gap); conditionnel (all uses); pronouns *y/en*; complex relatives (*lequel, auquel, dont*); concession/opposition (*bien que* + subj., *malgré, cependant, néanmoins*); purpose clauses; reported speech with tense backshift; passive voice; gérondif; participles.
- **Vocabulary:** Environment/climate, technology/social media, government/economy, and society/civic-life themes — these dominate TCF "fait de société" [social-issue] topics for Tâche 3 and Orale Tâche 3.
- **Listening/Reading:** Full-length timed TCF-format sets weekly, B2-tagged material.
- **Speaking:** Full 3-task Orale simulations, including Tâche 3 (*point de vue sans préparation* — opinion with no prep time, 4:30 min) — the task where most B1 candidates plateau. Drill structuring an argument (thesis + 2 reasons + example each + nuanced conclusion) in under 30 seconds of thinking.
- **Writing:** Full 3-task Écrite simulations under the real 60-minute clock; calibrate pacing (roughly 15/20/25 min per task).
- **TCF-specific:** One full timed mock of a rotating single skill, weekly.
- **Exit criteria:** 12/15+ on a mixed subjonctif/indicatif/conditionnel contrast drill · a full Écrite mock rated at B2 band on 2 of 3 tasks · an Orale Tâche 3 recording sustaining 3+ minutes of on-topic argued speech · CO/CE timed sets averaging 70%+ at B2 difficulty.

### Phase 4 — B2 + TCF Specialization (Weeks 15–18)

- **Objective:** Convert general B2 ability into exam-day reliability. No new grammar — pure error-elimination on whatever Phase 3's mocks actually surfaced.
- **Grammar:** Targeted review against the original error-patterns list; confirm each item is genuinely resolved, not just improved.
- **Vocabulary:** Consumerism, crime/safety, science, tourism, politics — fill remaining themes; shift from *learning* new words to *retrieving* known ones faster under pressure.
- **Listening/Reading:** Full official-format timed mocks, 2×/week each; review every wrong answer for the distractor pattern, not just the right answer.
- **Speaking:** 2 full Orale simulations/week on the real 12-minute clock (incl. 2 min prep); self-score against structure, register, and Tâche 3 spontaneity.
- **Writing:** 2 full Écrite simulations/week on the real 60-minute clock, targeting your two chronic failure modes directly (résumé omission, word-count discipline).
- **TCF-specific:** Learn exam-day logistics (booking, ID rules, interface). Given re-marking isn't currently available, treat every mock as the one real attempt.
- **Exit criteria:** 3 consecutive full Écrite mocks with correct word counts on all 3 tasks and no missing comparison on Tâche 3 · 2 consecutive full Orale mocks completed in full at real time · CO/CE mocks hitting the NCLC 7 raw-score band (458+/453+) at least twice each.

### Phase 5 — Mock Exams & Final Optimization (Weeks 19–21, flexible)

- **Objective:** Confirm exam-day readiness; find and fix your single weakest skill; taper.
- **All four skills:** 1–2 full, official-format, timed mocks under realistic conditions — ideally one full dress rehearsal at the real ~2h47 total sitting length.
- **TCF-specific:** Review your mock history across all skills. Remember: IRCC never averages. Whichever skill is lowest is your real constraint — spend the majority of remaining time there, not on your strongest skill.
- **Exit criteria (exam-ready signal):** 2 consecutive full mocks with all 4 skills independently at or above NCLC 7, *or* a clearly and consistently closing gap on whatever skill still lags.
- **Final week:** Light review only, no new material. Confirm booking, ID requirements, and test-centre/computer-based details.
